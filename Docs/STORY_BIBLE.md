# Story Bible: Untitled Sci-Fi Mining Game

## Core Premise

A father and daughter travel to hostile alien planets to mine resources needed to rebuild Earth after a devastating nuclear war. The story aims for **Interstellar-level emotional depth** - a narrative that lingers long after the credits roll.

---

## Primary Inspirations & What We're Taking From Each

### 1. Interstellar (2014)
- **Unspoken trust** between parent and child - Cooper and Murph don't need words
- The cosmic scale making human connections feel even more precious
- Time as a physical force that separates loved ones
- Science that feels grounded yet awe-inspiring
- The gut-punch of realizing what you've lost while saving others
- "Love is the one thing that transcends time and space"

### 2. The Walking Dead Game - Lee & Clementine
- Father and daughter **together** through the journey, not separated
- The protector dynamic - teaching survival while preserving innocence
- Every decision filtered through "what's best for her"
- Found family that becomes everything
- The weight of being someone's entire world

### 3. Arcane - Silco & Jinx
- **"Would burn the world for her"** - love without limits
- Silco's death scene: choosing her even at the end
- A father figure who is morally complex but genuine in his love
- The line: "You're perfect" - unconditional acceptance
- Love that others might see as toxic but is deeply real to them
- Protecting her from the world AND from herself

### 4. Attack on Titan
The user identified as a major fan. Key elements to incorporate:

**Narrative Techniques:**
- Hidden history revealed in layers (the basement reveal structure)
- PATHS concept - connected consciousness, future memories bleeding into present
- Moral complexity where no side is purely right
- The "chosen one" subverted - Eren wasn't special, he chose to be the monster
- History repeating because people don't learn

**Favorite Moments (referenced as inspiration):**
- Declaration of War
- Eren and Reiner's conversation in the basement (parallels, "we're the same")
- The Coordinate activation / Founding Titan reveal
- "If you want to save Mikasa and Armin... learn to control your power"
- Erwin's charge ("ADVANCE!")
- The reveal of the outside world
- Memory manipulation as plot device
- The weight of inherited trauma

## The Universe

### Earth - Post Nuclear War
- Nuclear war devastated the planet (cause TBD - human conflict? AI? External?)
- Survivors exist but resources are depleted
- The mining expeditions are humanity's lifeline
- Political factions likely fighting over remaining resources and expedition control

### The Edge of the Universe Concept
A planned major reveal/sequence:
- Characters travel to the literal edge of observable reality
- What they find: **parallel universes visible as spheres/balls arranged on a flat plane**
- The implication that their universe is one of infinite iterations
- Potential PATHS-like connection between versions?
- Could tie into the daughter seeing "memories" that aren't hers

### Alien Planets
- Hostile environments requiring suits/vehicles
- Indigenous alien life (enemies during mining operations)
- Resources unique to each planet needed for Earth's restoration
- Each planet could have its own biome/danger type

## Main Characters

### The Father
- Experienced miner/soldier - has done this work before the daughter joined
- Carries trauma from the war and/or previous missions
- His entire motivation is her survival and happiness
- Morally flexible when it comes to protecting her
- Potential Silco parallel: would he sacrifice others for her? Where's his line?

### The Daughter
- Age TBD (young enough to protect, old enough to be capable)
- Why is she on these missions? (Necessity? No one else to watch her? Her choice?)
- May have abilities/perceptions others don't (PATHS-style memories?)
- The emotional core - we see the world through her relationship with father
- Clementine parallel: learning to survive, maintaining hope/humanity

### Supporting Cast (To Be Developed)
- Other squad members (the soldiers we're building in the preview scene)
- Mission control / people back on Earth
- Potentially: other survivors encountered on planets
- Antagonists: human? alien? both?

## Thematic Pillars

### 1. Parental Love as Cosmic Force
Like Interstellar's "love transcends dimensions" - the father-daughter bond should be the emotional constant that grounds all the sci-fi spectacle.

### 2. What Would You Sacrifice?
Silco chose Jinx over his life's work. Cooper chose the mission over watching his kids grow. Where does our father draw the line?

### 3. Cycles and History
AoT's central theme - are we doomed to repeat? Does the daughter break a cycle the father couldn't?

### 4. Found Purpose in Devastation
In a ruined world, these two find meaning in each other.

## Key Scenes/Moments to Build Toward

1. **Opening** - Establish their dynamic, show why we should care
2. **First real danger** - Father's protective instincts in action
3. **The daughter proves herself** - She's not just cargo, she's capable
4. **Father's past revealed** - What is he carrying? What did he do in the war?
5. **A Silco moment** - Father does something morally questionable for her
6. **The Edge reveal** - Parallel universes, reality bigger than imagined
7. **Memory bleed** - Daughter experiences something she shouldn't know
8. **The choice** - Father must choose between her and something major
9. **Resolution** - Whatever it is, it should hurt (in a good way)

---

## Tone & Feel

- **Grounded sci-fi** - Technology feels used, worn, real
- **Quiet moments matter** - Not all tension, need breathing room
- **Earned emotion** - Build up so the payoffs land
- **Morally gray** - No pure villains, everyone has reasons
- **Hope exists** - Dark setting but not nihilistic
