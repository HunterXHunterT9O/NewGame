## Core Vision

### The Problem We're Solving
Sci-fi games often feel like "fantasy with laser swords" - surface-level aesthetics without the philosophical weight that makes great sci-fi literature and film so powerful. We want to change that.

### The Goal
Create a narrative-driven experience that:
- Achieves **Interstellar-level emotional depth**
- Has ideas that **linger after the credits roll**
- Features **moral weight** and **emotional resonance**
- Tells a story that couldn't exist in any other medium

### The Core Relationship
**A father and daughter, TOGETHER, on hostile alien planets.**

Not separated across time and space like Cooper and Murph. Together like Lee and Clementine. Every decision, every mission, every quiet moment - she's there. Watching. Learning. Judging. Depending on him.

## Primary Inspirations

### 1. Interstellar (2014)

**What We're Taking:**
- Cosmic scale making human connections feel even more precious
- Time as a physical force
- Science that feels grounded yet awe-inspiring
- The gut-punch of realizing what you've lost while saving others
- "Love is the one thing that transcends time and space"

**The Unspoken Trust (Cooper & Murph):**
- They never say "I trust you" - but every decision Cooper makes is rooted in getting back to her
- Murph's entire life becomes solving the equation her father believed in
- **Trust expressed through action across impossible distance**

**What We're NOT Copying:**
- The separation narrative (we're keeping them together)
- The ghost reveal (we need our own basement moment)
- Direct plot parallels

**Alternative Manifestations of Unspoken Trust:**

1. **Silence that says everything** - Two characters in a room. One is breaking down. The other doesn't offer words, doesn't touch them. Just stays. That *staying* is the trust.

2. **Inverse faith** - She stops looking for proof he's alive. Not because she's given up - because she doesn't *need* proof. She just knows. Others call it denial. It's not.

3. **The thing left unsaid** - He records a message for her. Pours his heart out. Deletes it. Sends three words instead: *"Still pointing home."* She understands everything.

4. **Parallel ritual** - Every night, he looks at the stars from whatever planet he's on. Same time, she looks up from Earth. Neither knows the other is doing it. But we, the audience, see it.

5. **Letting go as trust** - She makes a decision he would disagree with. He finds out. Says nothing. Trusts her to be her own person.

### 2. The Walking Dead Game (Lee & Clementine)

**Why Together Works for Games:**
- **Separation = Film language.** You watch someone yearn for reunion. It's passive.
- **Together = Game language.** You *experience* the relationship. Every decision feels witnessed.

**What Made Lee & Clem Work:**
- **She's always present** - you can't forget the stakes
- **She's watching** - your choices feel *witnessed*
- **She's growing** - she starts helpless, ends capable
- **You're preparing her** - every lesson is for the moment you won't be there
- **The dread is constant** - not "will I see her again?" but "will she survive *this*?"

**Applied to Our Story:**
- She's on the drop ship when you land on a hostile planet
- She sees you make the call to leave a squadmate behind
- She asks why the creatures scream when they die
- She finds something on a mission that changes how she sees you
- She starts finishing your sentences. Then she starts questioning your orders.

### 3. Arcane (Silco & Jinx)

**The Line That Defines Everything:**
> *"Is there anything so undoing as a daughter?"*

He's not complaining. He's not lamenting his weakness. He's **marveling** at it. He found the one thing that could break him, and he's *grateful.*

**"You're Perfect" - The Death Scene:**
Silco is dying. By her hand. And his last words aren't blame, aren't fear, aren't regret. Just... *"you're perfect."*

That's not a father forgiving his daughter. That's a father saying **there was never anything to forgive.**

**The Difference Between Lee and Silco:**
- **Lee's love is about letting go.** Preparing Clem for a world without him. Teaching her, then releasing her.
- **Silco's love is about never letting go.** He would burn Zaun to the ground. He would betray everything he built. He would die by her hand and thank her for it.

Lee says: *"I'm going to make sure you survive."*
Silco says: *"I would rather lose everything than lose you."*

**The Inheritance:**
Silco says the "undoing" line at Vander's statue because Vander understood too. Vander gave up the revolution for his kids. Silco hated him for it. And then became him. That's not irony. That's *inheritance.*

**How This Shapes Our Story:**

The father needs moments where he's offered the trade:
> *"Complete the mission. Earth's survival depends on it. But your daughter stays behind."*

And he doesn't hesitate. Not for a second. The answer is **no.**

Not "let me think about it."
Not "there has to be another way."
Just: **No.**

> **THEM:** "She's one child. Earth is billions of—"
> **HIM:** "I know what Earth is. And I know what she is."

**The Weight This Creates:**
A child who realizes their father would let the world burn for them... that's not a gift. That's a *weight.*

What if she starts pulling away? Not because she doubts his love - but because she loves him too much to be his undoing?

### 4. Attack on Titan

**Why AoT Changes Everything:**
AoT isn't about hope. It's about **the cost of freedom and the cycle of violence.** It asks:
- What if the "monsters" were victims first?
- What if the hero becomes the monster to save the people he loves?
- What if freedom requires becoming the very thing you hate?
- What if the truth is *worse* than the mystery?

And it earns its twists because **everything is seeded.** The basement. Eren's future memories. The Rumbling. You look back and realize it was always there.

## The Universe

### Earth - Post Nuclear War
- Nuclear war devastated the planet
- Survivors exist but resources are depleted
- Mining expeditions are humanity's lifeline
- Political factions likely fighting over remaining resources

**Potential Hidden Truth:**
The nuclear war wasn't nation vs nation. It was humanity trying to destroy something they found. They opened a door to another universe. Something came through. The bombs were meant to close it. They failed. The aliens aren't testing humanity - they're containing a mistake.

### The Aliens
**Initial Presentation:** Saviors who judged humanity for self-destruction and are giving them a chance at redemption through mining resources.

**Possible Deeper Truths:**
- They're not saviors - they're farmers. Humanity is livestock being trained.
- They ARE humanity - a future branch that left Earth millennia ago.
- They exist between universes, shepherds of the multiverse.
- They're also trapped. Doing this because something worse is coming.
- Humanity is a weapon being forged, not a species being saved.

### The Creatures
**Surface Level:** Hostile aliens defending their territory from mining operations.

**Deeper Truth Potential ("Eldians are humans" moment):**
- They're refugees from another universe
- They fled to these planets because *their* world was destroyed
- Humanity is doing to them what was done to Earth
- The things you've been killing aren't monsters - they're victims

### Alien Planets
- Hostile environments requiring suits/vehicles
- Indigenous life forms (the creatures)
- Resources unique to each planet
- Each planet has its own biome/danger type

## The Edge of the Universe (PATHS)

### The Vision
> *"Going to the edge of the universe and seeing what's outside, only for it to be parallel universes stacked together as balls on a flat plane."*

This is the "PATHS" of our story. A visual and conceptual space that changes everything.

### How It Works

**What PATHS does in AoT:**
- Connects all Eldians across time
- Allows memory transfer (future AND past)
- Is where Ymir exists, building Titans for 2,000 years
- Is where the truth is revealed
- Is where choices are made that echo across all of history

**What the Edge does in our story:**
- The parallel universes (spheres) are visible - every possible timeline
- The father can SEE other versions of himself, his daughter, humanity
- Time doesn't work here - he can witness past and future
- The aliens exist HERE, between universes - shepherds of the multiverse
- The TRUTH lives here - why they were really chosen, what humanity really did

### The Revelation Scene (Late Act 2 / Early Act 3)

The father is on a mission that goes wrong. He's separated from his squad, drifting in a damaged vessel toward what the instruments say is... nothing. The edge of mapped space.

But he doesn't die. He passes *through*.

And he sees it.

**Spheres. Countless. Each one a universe.** Floating on an infinite flat plane that stretches beyond comprehension. Some are bright. Some are dim. Some are dark - dead.

He sees Earth's sphere. Small. Flickering.

And next to it, almost touching, another sphere where Earth is *whole*. Where the war never happened. Where his daughter grew up with a father.

He could reach out. He could—

But he can't. He's pulled back. The aliens retrieve him.

**The Truth:** This isn't about redemption. It's about which sphere survives. The aliens aren't saving humanity out of mercy. They're trying to prevent Earth's sphere from going dark - because when one dies, it destabilizes the others. It's cosmic triage. Humanity isn't special. It's just... *next* to something that is.

## Main Characters

### The Father

**Who He Is:**
- Experienced miner/soldier
- Carries trauma from the war and/or previous missions
- His entire motivation is her survival and happiness
- Morally flexible when it comes to protecting her
- Would choose her over Earth. Every time. Without hesitation.

**The "Erwin Dream" - His Selfish Core:**
Every compelling protagonist has a selfish dream wrapped in noble purpose. Erwin wanted to reach the basement not to save humanity - but to prove his father's theory right.

What's our father's selfish dream?
- Is he doing this to save Earth? Or to prove he's not the man who failed his family before?
- Does he *need* to be the hero because he can't live with being ordinary?
- Is the daughter his redemption... or his excuse?

**Potential Hidden Sin:**
- He caused the war. Or couldn't stop it.
- The survivors trust this soldier to save them. He doesn't trust himself.
- Redemption isn't about being forgiven. It's about *earning* the right to exist.

### The Daughter

**The Central Question:** How old is she?
- **Young (8-10):** More vulnerable, dependent, "protect at all costs." Moral compass that keeps him human.
- **Adolescent (13-15):** Starting to push back, form own opinions. Can help but also challenges.

**Why Is She With Him?**
- Did the aliens take them both?
- Was she the reason he was selected? ("We don't want soldiers. We want someone with something to lose.")
- Is she the only family he has left, so he refused to leave without her?

**Her Role in Gameplay:**
- AI companion you issue orders to (like Ellie in TLOU)
- Someone you protect during extraction
- A resource - she can do things you can't (small spaces, distractions, hacking?)
- Does she grow mechanically? Start useless, end competent?

**Her Arc:**
She starts as protected. She ends as... what?
- His equal?
- His replacement?
- The one who breaks the cycle he couldn't?

**The Forest Question:**
Sasha's father says the adults must pull the children out of the forest - out of the cycle of hatred.

The father is IN the forest. He's killing creatures on other worlds. He's teaching his daughter to survive through violence. He's perpetuating the cycle.

Maybe she's the one who sees it first. She befriends a creature. She refuses to kill. She says:
> "Baba, when does it stop? When do WE stop?"

And he doesn't have an answer.

### The Symbol: The Compass

Like Interstellar's watch - the physical manifestation of their bond.

- Given to her during the cold open
- "This will point you home. And home is wherever I come back to."
- Appears in UI (loading screens, menus)
- In the final act, the needle moves. It's not magnetic. It's pointing to her. Always has been.

## Story Structure

### COLD OPEN - "The Promise"

*No logos. No title. No music. Just black.*

*Sound fades in: a child's breathing. Shaky. Scared.*

*We see hands - small hands - clutching a worn stuffed rabbit.*

*A man's voice, calm but strained:*

> **"Don't look outside, habibti. Look at me."**

*The child looks up. We don't see the father's face yet - just his silhouette against a window filled with orange light. Distant thunder that isn't thunder.*

> **"I'm going to fix this. But I need you to wait for me. Can you do that?"**

*The child nods.*

> **"Say it."**

*Small voice:*

> **"I'll wait."**

> **"No matter how long?"**

> **"...No matter how long."**

*He presses something into her hand - a small, battered compass. Closes her fingers around it.*

> **"This will point you home. And home is wherever I come back to."**

*He stands. Walks toward the light. The child calls out:*

> **"Baba?"**

*He stops. Doesn't turn.*

> **"Come back."**

*Beat.*

> **"That's not a promise I can make. But I can promise I'll try."**

*He walks into the light. The child clutches the compass. The orange glow intensifies until—*

**SMASH CUT TO BLACK.**

**TITLE: [GAME NAME]**

**TEXT: "32 years later."**

*We open on a woman's hands. Weathered. Scarred. Holding the same compass.*

---

### ACT 1: "The Selection" (~15%)

**After the cold open, we're with the father.**

- He didn't walk into death - he walked into an alien vessel
- The aliens selected humans they deemed "capable of change"
- He's been in stasis. To him, it's been days. To Earth, decades.
- He learns the deal: mine resources, prove humanity can build instead of destroy
- **The twist:** He learns his daughter is alive. She's an adult now. She thinks he's dead.
- But he can't contact her. Not yet. He has to earn it.

*Gameplay: First mining mission. Tutorial framed as his first deployment.*

**But wait - we're keeping them together.**

Alternative: The cold open is a flashback/prologue. The main game starts with them ALREADY together on missions. The "how" is revealed through gameplay/dialogue.

---

### ACT 2: "The Cost" (~60%)

**The missions. The grind. The erosion.**

- Each mission takes place on a different hostile planet
- Squad members have their own "Murphs" - people they're fighting for
- Some lose them. Some learn they've been forgotten. Some break.
- **The creatures aren't mindless.** Evidence emerges they're intelligent. Defending their home.
- **The moral fracture:** Are we heroes or colonizers?
- Father prioritizes daughter over mission. People die. He's confronted.

**Key Moment:**
> **THEM:** "You had a choice. You chose wrong."
> **HIM:** *(quiet)* "I chose the only thing I could."
> **THEM:** "She's one child. Earth is billions of—"
> **HIM:** "I know what Earth is. And I know what she is."

*Gameplay escalates: Harder planets, tougher choices, squad permadeath, resource quotas.*

---

### ACT 3: "The Return" (~25%)

**The final mission. The reckoning.**

- Earth has enough resources. One last extraction.
- But the father has learned something at the Edge
- **The revelation** (the basement moment - choose one):
  - The aliens are farmers, humanity is livestock
  - The aliens ARE future humanity
  - The resources are a tithe, a debt, forever
  - Humanity caused more than just the war
- **The choice:** Complete the mission... or break the cycle
- **The Interstellar moment:** The compass finds its way back to her. Maybe with a message. Maybe just the object itself.

## Key Scenes & Moments to Build

1. **Opening** - Establish their dynamic in action
2. **First real danger** - Father's protective instincts, daughter witnesses violence
3. **The daughter proves herself** - She's not cargo, she's capable
4. **Squad bonding** - Other characters we care about losing
5. **Father's past revealed** - What is he carrying?
6. **A Silco moment** - Father does something morally questionable for her
7. **Creature intelligence reveal** - Are we the monsters?
8. **The Edge of the Universe** - PATHS moment, multiverse revealed
9. **Memory bleed** - Daughter experiences something she shouldn't know
10. **The choice** - Father chooses between her and something major
11. **The confrontation** - Squad/aliens confront him about his choices
12. **Her growth** - She surpasses him in some way
13. **Breaking the cycle** - She does what he couldn't
14. **Resolution** - The compass, the ending, the thing that stays with players

## Thematic Pillars

### 1. Parental Love as Cosmic Force
Like Interstellar's "love transcends dimensions" - the father-daughter bond is the emotional constant that grounds all the sci-fi spectacle.

### 2. What Would You Sacrifice?
Silco chose Jinx over his life's work. Cooper chose the mission over watching his kids grow. Where does our father draw the line? (Answer: He doesn't. She wins. Every time.)

### 3. Cycles and History
AoT's central theme - are we doomed to repeat? The daughter might be the one who breaks a cycle the father couldn't.

### 4. The Forest
> "Someone has to pull the children out of the forest."

Can you save someone by teaching them violence? Or are you just leading them deeper into the forest?

### 5. Monsters and Victims
Nothing is simple. The creatures might be refugees. The aliens might be trapped too. The father might be both savior and destroyer.

### 6. "I Reject Their Wish"
Eren's defining moment - choosing himself over what others want him to be. Our protagonist needs this energy.

## Tone & Feel

- **Grounded sci-fi** - Technology feels used, worn, real
- **Quiet moments matter** - Not all tension, need breathing room
- **Earned emotion** - Build up so payoffs land
- **Morally gray** - No pure villains, everyone has reasons
- **Hope exists** - Dark setting but not nihilistic
- **Cool factor counts** - Cart Titan with armor energy. Dark Descent energy.

---

## What Makes It NOT Generic

| Generic Approach | Our Approach |
|------------------|--------------|
| Aliens are evil monsters | Aliens are native inhabitants - *you're* the invader |
| Military bravado | Soldiers with doubts, trauma, conflicting loyalties |
| "Save Earth" is unquestioned | Characters questioning if Earth *deserves* saving |
| Clear heroes and villains | Everyone has reasons; no one is purely right |
| Father-daughter separated | Father-daughter together, relationship witnessed |
| Straightforward redemption | Redemption requiring becoming what you despise |

## Open Questions

### Story
- What caused the nuclear war? (Accident? AI? First contact gone wrong?)
- What exactly did humanity "find" that they tried to destroy?
- What is the daughter's "ability" if any? (Memory bleed? PATHS connection?)
- Who/what are the aliens really?
- Is there a human antagonist faction?
- How does the parallel universe concept connect to the main plot?
- What is the father's backstory/sin?
- Does he die? (The Lee question)
- How does it end?

### Character
- Daughter's exact age and personality
- Father's name (the "Baba" suggests Middle Eastern background)
- Squad members - who are they? What are their "Murphs"?
- The aliens - do they have individual characters or are they a collective?

### Design
- How explicit is the story? (Cutscenes? Environmental? Emergent?)
- Player agency in story beats?
- Multiple endings?
- How dark can we go?
